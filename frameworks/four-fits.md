# Four Fits Growth Diagnostic

*Based on Brian Balfour's "Why Product Market Fit Isn't Enough"*

## Core Principle

**Product-market fit is necessary but not sufficient.** Scaling requires four interlocking fits: Market-Product, Product-Channel, Channel-Model, and Model-Market. Companies where all four align grow smoothly. Companies missing any fit grind – applying more effort for diminishing returns.

When growth stalls, the bottleneck is almost always a fit mismatch, not a tactics problem.

---

## The Four Fits

```
Market ←→ Product ←→ Channel ←→ Model
  ↑_________________________________↑
```

The fits form a loop. Each element must fit with its neighbours, and the loop must close (Model-Market).

### 1. Market-Product Fit

**Does the product solve a real problem for a defined market?**

| Signal of fit | Signal of misfit |
|---|---|
| Customers pull the product out of you | You push the product at customers |
| Word-of-mouth referrals | Heavy sales effort for every deal |
| Usage grows without prompting | Usage requires constant customer success intervention |
| Customers describe their problem in your language | You have to educate customers on why they need this |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- Can you describe the target customer in one sentence?
- Can the customer describe their problem without you coaching them?
- Would customers be upset if you took the product away?

### 2. Product-Channel Fit

**Is the product designed for the channel that reaches your target?**

Products must be built for their distribution channel – you can't bolt on virality or enterprise sales after the fact.

| Channel | Product requirements |
|---|---|
| Viral/word-of-mouth | Product must have sharing built into the core loop. Value increases with more users. |
| Content/SEO | Product must generate or enable content that ranks. |
| Self-serve/PLG | Product must deliver value without human assistance. Free tier or trial required. |
| Inside sales | Product must demo well in 30 minutes. Clear ROI story. |
| Enterprise/field sales | Product must support pilots, custom config, security reviews. Multi-stakeholder value. |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- What channel reaches your target customer? (Not: what channel do you prefer?)
- Is your product actually built for that channel, or are you forcing it?
- Can a new user get value through the channel without talking to anyone?

### 3. Channel-Model Fit

**Does the revenue model support the cost of the channel?**

High-touch channels require high ACVs. Low-touch channels require high volume. The math must work.

| Channel | Required ACV range | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Viral/self-serve | < $1K/yr | No sales cost to recover |
| Inside sales | $5K – $25K/yr | Telesales team cost per deal |
| Field sales | $25K – $500K+/yr | Travel, demos, multi-touch sales cycles |
| Enterprise | $100K+/yr | Long sales cycles, solutions engineering, legal |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- What does it cost to acquire a customer through your primary channel?
- Does your ACV cover that cost with enough margin?
- If you raised prices to cover channel costs, would customers still buy?

### 4. Model-Market Fit

**Does the market support the revenue model at scale?**

The loop closes here. The market must be large enough, with enough potential customers at the right price point, to support the business.

| Model | Market requirement |
|---|---|
| High volume, low ACV | Very large addressable market (millions of potential customers) |
| Medium volume, medium ACV | Tens of thousands of potential customers |
| Low volume, high ACV | Thousands of potential customers, each worth $100K+ |

**Diagnostic questions:**
- How many potential customers exist at your target ACV?
- Is the market growing, stable, or shrinking?
- Can you reach enough of them through your channel?

---

## Using the Diagnostic

### When growth stalls

Walk through each fit in order. The first misfit you find is your bottleneck.

1. **Market-Product**: Are customers pulling, or are you pushing?
2. **Product-Channel**: Is the product built for the channel you're using?
3. **Channel-Model**: Does the ACV cover the channel cost?
4. **Model-Market**: Is the market big enough at this price point?

### When considering a strategic change

Changing one fit forces you to re-evaluate all four. Common examples:

| Change | Ripple effects |
|---|---|
| Moving upmarket | Product needs enterprise features → channel shifts to field sales → ACV must increase → market shrinks |
| Adding PLG motion | Product needs self-serve onboarding → channel shifts to content/viral → ACV drops → market must expand |
| New product line | New market segment → new product-channel requirements → may need different model |

### When evaluating a new feature or product

Before building, trace through all four fits:
- Who is the market? (Market-Product)
- How will they discover this? (Product-Channel)
- Can we afford that distribution? (Channel-Model)
- Is the market large enough? (Model-Market)

## Four Fits vs. Other Frameworks

| Framework | Focus | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Four Fits** | Why growth is stalling across the full loop | Diagnosing growth bottlenecks, evaluating strategic pivots |
| **Barbell Strategy** | Where to allocate resources (safe vs. high-upside) | Resource allocation decisions |
| **Next Feature Fallacy** | Whether a specific feature will move retention | Feature-level prioritisation |
| **JTBD / Switching Threshold** | Whether customers will switch to your product | Understanding purchase decisions |

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Optimising one fit while ignoring the loop**: Building the perfect product for a market you can't reach through any affordable channel
- **Assuming fits are static**: Markets shift, channels mature, competitors change pricing. Re-evaluate quarterly.
- **Copying another company's fit stack**: Dropbox's viral + freemium model worked because of their market. Your market may require enterprise sales.
- **Blaming tactics when the fit is wrong**: More SDRs won't help if the product isn't built for inside sales. More content won't help if the product doesn't self-serve.
