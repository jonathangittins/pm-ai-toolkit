# The Taste Gap

*Based on Ira Glass's advice on creative work*

## Core Principle

**Your taste develops faster than your ability.** When you start doing creative work – writing specs, running customer calls, presenting strategy, building products – you can tell your output isn't as good as you want it to be. That's not a sign you're in the wrong job. It's a sign your taste is ahead of your skill. The gap closes through volume, not study.

---

## The Progression

```
Phase 1: Good taste, poor output     → You know it's not good enough. Painful.
Phase 2: Good taste, improving output → Occasionally you hit the mark. Encouraging.
Phase 3: Good taste, matched output   → Your work consistently meets your standard.
Phase 4: Refined taste, seeking edge  → Your standard keeps rising. Back to Phase 1.
```

Phase 1 is where most people quit. They interpret the gap as evidence of inability rather than evidence of developing taste.

---

## How the Gap Closes

### Volume, not study

Reading about how to write a good spec doesn't close the gap. Writing 30 specs does. Each one is a rep. Most will be mediocre. Some will be good. A few will be great. The ratio improves with volume.

### Deadlines force output

Without external pressure, the taste gap becomes an excuse to endlessly refine. Deadlines – weekly reviews, monthly cadences, quarterly specs – force you to ship work that isn't perfect. That's the mechanism.

### Feedback accelerates the cycle

Volume alone works, but volume + feedback works faster. Show your work to someone whose taste you trust. Their reaction tells you which parts of the gap you're closing and which persist.

---

## Where This Applies

| Domain | What "volume" looks like |
|---|---|
| Writing specs | Ship one per feature, don't over-polish. Review past specs quarterly – you'll see improvement. |
| Customer calls | Run 3-5 per week. The first 50 are mostly practice. |
| Strategy presentations | Present monthly, even informally. Record yourself and watch back. |
| Slack communication | Write daily updates. The muscle develops through repetition. |
| Product judgment | Make decisions, track outcomes. Pattern recognition builds through reps, not theory. |

---

## The Trap

The taste gap can become a permanent excuse if you let it:

- "I'll write the spec when I know more" → You'll never know enough. Write it now.
- "I'm not ready to present to leadership" → Present anyway. The gap closes through doing.
- "My product sense isn't good enough yet" → It's built through decisions, not preparation.

The gap is a feature, not a bug. It means your standards are high. Now close it through volume.
