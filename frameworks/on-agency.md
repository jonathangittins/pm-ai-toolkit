# On Agency

*Based on Henrik Karlsson's "On Agency" (Escaping Flatland)*

## Core Principle

**Agency is not a fixed trait – it's a compound skill built through a feedback loop.** Take a small action → the world responds → update your model of what's possible → take a slightly larger action. Over time, this builds a general expectation that problems are solvable, which is the defining characteristic of high-agency people.

---

## Two Components

Agency is an amalgamation of two dispositions:

### 1. Autonomy

The capacity to formulate your own goals – to dig inside and figure out what wants to happen through you, regardless of how strange or wrong it seems to others. This means distinguishing between goals you've chosen and goals you've inherited from social pressure, convention, or "what people in my role are supposed to do."

**The opposite**: Doing what you're "supposed to do." Playing social games that don't align with what, on reflection, seems valuable.

### 2. Efficacy

The ability and willingness to pursue those goals. The drive to see reality as it is – not as you wish it were – so you can manipulate it effectively. This means treating problems as solvable by default, rather than assuming they're someone else's job or fundamentally unsolvable.

**The opposite**: Being passive in the face of problems. Assuming things can't change. Waiting for permission or for someone else to act.

---

## How Agency Develops

Agency is contextual – someone bold in one domain can be passive in another. It develops through:

1. **Noticing**: High-agency people notice problems others walk past. Develop the habit of writing down what's broken, even if you can't fix it yet.
2. **Small bets**: Take a small action on something that seems stuck. Send the email. Write the proposal. Run the experiment. The outcome – good or bad – updates your model.
3. **Tracking results**: Agency compounds when you have evidence that your actions change outcomes. Keep track of what you tried and what happened.
4. **Environment**: Surroundings that punish initiative kill agency. Surroundings that reward small experiments grow it. Choose your environment deliberately.

---

## Agency Is Not Aggression

A common misconception: high agency means being hard-edged, type-A, forcing outcomes. In practice, agency is often gentle – an attunement to the world and the self, a feeling out of reality, and finding the path of least resistance. There's sometimes considerable force involved, but it's the force of a river being pulled toward the sea, not a bulldozer.

This matters because the aggressive framing causes people to dismiss agency as "not my style." Agency doesn't require a particular personality. It requires noticing what's true and acting on it.

---

## Personal Diagnostics

### Signs of growing agency
- You notice problems before others raise them
- When something seems stuck, your default response is to try something
- You can articulate goals that are genuinely yours, not inherited
- You've stopped waiting for permission to start things
- Failed attempts update your model rather than discourage you

### Signs of shrinking agency
- You know something is wrong but assume someone else will fix it
- Your goals feel like obligations rather than choices
- You avoid problems that seem "too hard" or "not your job"
- You're spending time on things that feel safe rather than things that matter
- You haven't tried anything new in a while

---

## How to Cultivate Agency

1. **Audit your goals**: Which of your current goals did you choose? Which did you inherit? This isn't about rejecting all external expectations – it's about knowing which ones you've genuinely endorsed vs. which ones you're following by default.
2. **Lower the stakes**: Agency doesn't start with bold moves. It starts with sending the Slack message, scheduling the meeting, writing the first draft. Make the smallest possible move on the thing you've been avoiding.
3. **Protect your agency environment**: If your team or process punishes initiative (every proposal needs three approvals, every experiment needs justification), that's an agency-hostile environment. Either change it or work around it.
4. **Build an evidence log**: Keep a running list of times you acted and it worked. Agency compounds when you have proof that your actions matter.
