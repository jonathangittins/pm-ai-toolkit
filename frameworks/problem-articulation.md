# Strategic Problem Articulation

*Based on @nurijanian's consultant framework*

## Core Principle

**When you can articulate a complex problem in two sentences, you've done 80% of the strategic work.** Most PMs either start with dashboards (data-driven) or start with hypotheses (strategy-driven). Elite PMs run both and surface where they disagree – that conflict is where the real insight lives.

---

## The Problem

Most PMs unconsciously favour one mode:

| Mode | Starts with | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Data-driven** (inductive) | Dashboards, metrics, user behaviour | Finds patterns but misses strategic context. "The data says X" without asking "should X matter?" |
| **Strategy-driven** (deductive) | Hypotheses, first principles, market logic | Builds elegant logic but cherry-picks data to confirm it. Motivated reasoning. |

The gap: when your strategic logic says "build feature X" but your data says "users want feature Y" – most PMs resolve that tension by hiding it. They pick a side and present it as obvious.

---

## The 6-Step Framework

### 1. Simplify
Reduce the problem to 2-3 decision factors. If you can't articulate it in two sentences, you don't understand it yet.

**Test**: Can you explain the core tension to someone outside your team in under 30 seconds?

### 2. Deduct (top-down)
Build a logic tree from first principles and strategy. Given what we know about our market, customers, and competitive position – what *should* the answer be?

### 3. Induct (bottom-up)
Gather evidence from data, user behaviour, and market signals. What does the evidence *actually* say, independent of your strategic hypothesis?

### 4. Synthesise
Surface conflicts explicitly. Where do steps 2 and 3 disagree? Don't resolve the tension prematurely – name it.

**The question**: "When did your data last contradict your strategy, and what did you do about it?" If you can't remember, you're probably hiding the tension.

### 5. Decide
Make a clear choice while acknowledging the tension. "We're going with X despite Y evidence because Z." Not "the data clearly shows X" when it doesn't clearly show anything.

### 6. Recommend
Structure for executive trust. Consultants win because they make their thinking process legible and auditable. Executives trust recommendations when they can see the reasoning, not just the conclusion.

---

## When to Use This Framework

- ✅ Preparing a recommendation for leadership
- ✅ Resolving competing priorities between teams
- ✅ Any decision where data and strategy point in different directions
- ✅ Framing docs and decision memos

Skip it for:
- ❌ Tactical decisions where the data is clear and unambiguous
- ❌ Decisions that don't need executive buy-in

Principle #3 (metrics-informed not metrics-driven) is the mindset. This framework is the method for actually doing it.

### Connection to Communication Guidelines
Step 6 (Recommend) directly applies the communication rules – lead with the recommendation, structure logically, be specific.

### Connection to Decision Speed Framework

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ **Presenting synthesis as obvious** – If you skip step 4, you're hiding the tension, not resolving it
- ❌ **Using data to justify a predetermined conclusion** – Evidence collection should test strategy, not confirm it
- ❌ **Over-simplifying in step 1** – Two sentences doesn't mean removing nuance. It means finding the core tension.
- ❌ **Skipping the deductive step** – "Let's just look at the data" sounds rigorous but misses strategic context
